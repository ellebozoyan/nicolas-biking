Every part of a bike has a name and a job. Knowing the parts helps you understand how to ride, how to fix small problems, and what to ask for at the shop.

The parts and what they do

Helmet ⭐ The MOST important piece. Two finger widths above the eyebrows, straps in a V around the ears, snug under the chin. Wear it EVERY ride."Helmet on first."
Frame The metal triangle that holds everything together. Has a top tube, down tube, seat tube, and stays."The skeleton."
Wheels & tires Two of them. Tires need air — squeeze them every ride. If you can squish them easy, they need a pump."Check the air."
Spokes The thin wires from the hub to the rim of each wheel. Like a wagon wheel."Keep them clear."
Handlebars What you hold to steer. Two hands on the grips. Keep them light — don't squeeze too hard."Light hands."
Brake levers On the handlebars. Right hand = REAR brake (usually). Left hand = FRONT brake (usually). Squeeze BOTH together to stop smoothly."Both levers together."
Seat (saddle) Where you sit. Should be at the height where your leg is almost straight when the pedal is at the bottom."Almost straight leg."
Pedals What you push with your feet. Push DOWN through the ball of your foot, not the arch."Ball of foot."
Crank The arms that hold the pedals. They turn around in circles to drive the chain."Round and round."
Chain The metal links that connect the crank to the rear wheel. Don't touch with bare hands — chains get greasy."Greasy. Don't touch."

Biking has stages. Each stage opens up new places to ride. There is no rush. Every rider started where you are.

🟡

Driveway Stage

What you learn here: balance, starting, stopping, steering. The driveway is the perfect classroom. No traffic, no surprises.

"Master the basics."
🛤️

Path Stage

What you learn here: riding on bike paths and quiet streets. Hand signals. Riding next to Daddy. Longer distances.

"Stay smart, stay seen."
🌲

What you learn here: riding on dirt, going over roots and rocks, climbing hills, descending. Your brain reads the trail one move ahead.

"Eyes ahead."

What you learn here: longer rides, route planning, basic maintenance, riding with friends. By now your skills, your safety habits, and your spirit are all yours.

"My bike. My ride. Forever."

Helmet fit, hand signals, road rules — kid-friendly version.

Biking has clear rules — for safety AND for fun. Once you know them, every ride is better. These keep you safe on the driveway, the bike path, and the road.
⭐

1. Helmet on, every ride

No helmet = no riding. Two finger widths above the eyebrows, straps in a V around the ears, snug under the chin (one finger between strap and chin). Replace after any crash.

"Helmet first. Always." ▶ Watch
🚦

2. Ride WITH traffic

On the road, ride in the SAME direction as cars. Stay as far right as is safe. Single file when riding with someone else.

"Same way as cars." ▶ Watch
🛑

3. Stop at stop signs

Bikes follow car rules. STOP at every stop sign. Look LEFT, then RIGHT, then LEFT AGAIN before crossing.

"Look both ways twice." ▶ Watch
✋

4. Hand signals

LEFT turn: stick left arm STRAIGHT out. RIGHT turn: left arm BENT up at the elbow (or right arm straight out). STOP: left arm bent DOWN at the elbow.

"Tell people what you're doing." ▶ Watch
👁️

5. Look before you turn

Always look OVER YOUR SHOULDER before changing direction. Then signal. Then turn. Look-signal-turn — that order.

"Look. Signal. Turn." ▶ Watch
🪞

6. Stay seen

Wear bright colors (especially yellow). Use lights at night or in the rain. Make eye contact with drivers at intersections.

"Bright clothes. Eye contact." ▶ Watch
🚸

7. Sidewalks & bike paths

On the sidewalk: SLOW DOWN. Walkers always have right of way. Say "On your left!" before passing. On bike paths: same — slow down for walkers.

"Say 'on your left.'" ▶ Watch
🤲

8. Two hands on the bars

No riding "no hands" until you're MUCH more experienced. Keep both hands on the handlebars, ready to brake.

"Two hands. Always." ▶ Watch
🛞

9. Bike check before EVERY ride

A-B-C check: Air in the tires (squeeze them). Brakes work (squeeze the levers). Chain on (look down). Takes 30 seconds. Saves crashes.

"A. B. C. Then go." ▶ Watch
🤝

10. Be a great rider

Wave to other riders. Help if someone falls. Don't show off to make others feel small. Biking is more fun when everyone is welcome.

"Wave. Help. Be kind." ▶ Watch

Every move on a bike has a name and a feel. You already know how to balance. Now let's name the moves and learn the secrets.

The 6 essential moves

🚀

Starting (the push-off)

Pedal up to the 2 o'clock position. Foot ON the pedal. Push DOWN as you push off with the other foot. The down-push is what gets you going.

"Pedal at 2. Push down." ▶ Watch
🛑

Stopping (smooth)

Squeeze BOTH brake levers TOGETHER — equal pressure on each. Slide back on the seat. Foot down only AFTER you've stopped.

"Both brakes. Foot last." ▶ Watch
↩️

Turning

LOOK where you want to go (your body follows your eyes). Lean slightly into the turn. Smooth gentle steer — don't yank the bars.

"Look. Lean. Glide." ▶ Watch
⚖️

Balancing slow

Looking ahead helps balance more than looking down. The slower you go, the harder it gets. Keep some pedal pressure on at all times.

"Eyes up. Keep pedaling." ▶ Watch
⚙️

Shifting gears

LOW gear (easy) = for hills going UP. HIGH gear (hard) = for flat or downhill. Shift while pedaling. Don't shift while standing still.

"Up = low. Flat = high." ▶ Watch
✋

Signaling

Stick your arm out for left turn. Bent up for right turn. Bent down for stop. Practice in the driveway before you need them.

"Tell people first." ▶ Watch
▶ Watch

The Helmet Check (every ride)

The 2-V-1 check:

  • 2 fingers above your eyebrows — that's how high the front of the helmet sits.
  • V shape — the straps come together to form a V right under your earlobes.
  • 1 finger between the chin strap and your chin — snug, but not choking.
"2, V, 1. Every ride."

The Athletic Bike Position

Before every ride, find this position:

  • Pedals at 3 and 9 o'clock (level).
  • Knees slightly bent.
  • Elbows slightly bent (don't lock).
  • Eyes UP, looking 20+ feet ahead.
  • Butt slightly off the seat for bumps.
"Knees soft. Eyes far."

The Five Steps of Every Stop

1️⃣

See it coming

Look ahead. If you might need to stop, START thinking about it now.

"Plan ahead." ▶ Watch
2️⃣

Signal

Stick your hand out (the STOP signal) so others know.

"Tell them." ▶ Watch
3️⃣

Squeeze BOTH brakes

Equal pressure on both levers. Front brake does most of the stopping; rear keeps you stable.

"Both together." ▶ Watch
4️⃣

Shift weight back

Slide your butt slightly back on the seat. This keeps the front wheel grounded.

"Weight back." ▶ Watch
5️⃣

Foot down

Only after the bike has STOPPED. Pick your stronger leg. Put that foot flat on the ground.

"Foot last." ▶ Watch

The structure stays the same so Nicolas can predict what's coming. The content inside the structure changes so it never gets boring. Four lesson formats roll in over the year — one at a time, never all at once. Each format scales to a 60-min lesson with Coach or a 30-min practice with Daddy.

Why formats, not single-skill weeks

Nicolas needs predictable structure. He also needs novelty — but novelty inside the structure, not novelty AS the structure. So instead of "this week we do uppies, next week we do dribbles," every practice follows the same shape: warm up → play → new skill → play with skill → drill → clean up. The shape is the constant. What goes inside each block is what evolves.

Built-in transitions (water cooler, ball machine adjustments, lights on/off) are part of the practice, not interruptions. They reset attention between blocks. Plan for them.

The Four Formats

One format starts. A second is added at week 7. A third at week 27. A fourth at week 40. After each addition, formats rotate — never converge.

The Warm-Up Menu

These are the foundation moves from the original "weeks 1–6." They never go away. They live in the warm-up of every format, every session — picking 1–3 each time, rotating through. New foundation moves get added; old ones fade and return.

⭐ Helmet Check

2 fingers above eyebrows. V shape at ears. 1 finger under the chin. Every ride.

"2, V, 1."

🛞 ABC Bike Check

Air in tires. Brakes work. Chain on. 30 seconds, then ride.

"A. B. C."

🤸 Body Wake-Up

Arm circles, leg swings, hip openers, ankle circles. 30 seconds each.

"Wake the body."

8️⃣ Figure-8s

Two cones 10 feet apart. Ride a figure-8 around them. Slow first, then faster. Builds steering and balance.

"Look where you go."

🐢 Slow Race

LAST to finish wins. Stay upright while going as slowly as possible. Builds balance.

"Slower is harder."

🦵 One-Foot Pedal

Pedal in a straight line using only ONE foot for 10 seconds. Switch. Builds smooth pedal strokes.

"Smooth circles."

👀 Shoulder Check

Ride straight forward. Look BACK over your left shoulder. Then your right. Stay straight while you look.

"Look. Stay straight."

✋ Signal Practice

Ride with one hand on the bars. Stick out the other arm — left turn signal. Hold for 5 seconds. Switch hands.

"One hand. Steady."

🛑 Controlled Stops

Ride toward a cone. STOP with the front wheel right at the cone. Use both brakes. Foot down ONLY when stopped.

"Both brakes. Foot last."

🚧 Cone Slalom

5 cones in a line, 4 feet apart. Weave through them. Smooth turns. Don't hit the cones.

"Smooth and slow."

The 52-Week Rotation

Each week shows the format used and the new content focus inside it. Skills can rotate, fade, return, or be skipped. Life happens — there is no falling behind.

Phase 1 · Discover the Bike

Weeks 1–6 Format A only Same shape every time

Six weeks of the exact same lesson shape. Every block in the same order. The content inside the warm-up and the new-skill block rotates through the foundation moves so Nicolas gets familiar with all of them — but the structure never moves. This is when the format becomes predictable. After six weeks, he knows it.

Week 1A

Discover the Bike

Warm-up: body wake-up + helmet check. New skill: name every part of the bike. Play: "name that part" game with Daddy.

Week 2A

The Helmet & Fit

Warm-up: helmet check. New skill: the 2-V-1 helmet fit check. Also: bike fit (seat height — leg almost straight at the bottom pedal).

Week 3A

Discover Balance

Warm-up: body wake-up. New skill: coasting in a straight line (no pedaling). Eyes up, soft knees. 20 feet without putting a foot down.

Week 4A

Discover Starting

Warm-up: helmet + ABC check. New skill: the push-off — pedal at 2 o'clock, foot ON, push DOWN. Smooth start.

Week 5A

Discover Stopping

Warm-up: ABC check. New skill: both brakes together. Foot down ONLY when stopped. Practice stopping at a cone.

Week 6A

Discover Steering

Warm-up: figure-8s. New skill: look where you want to go. Smooth turns, no yanking the bars.

Phase 2 · Adding Riding Skills

Weeks 7–26 A & B alternating First format change

Format B joins. The two formats alternate every other week — A, then B, then A, then B. Format A keeps introducing new technical pieces (routes, throws, catches); Format B applies them in cooperative drills. Watch for Nicolas noticing the new format. Talk about it. Use the visual schedule to preview the difference.

Week 7A

Braking Technique

New skill: front brake does most of the stopping. Rear keeps you stable. Squeeze BOTH evenly.

Week 8B

Braking Drill

Ride at speed. Daddy yells "STOP!" Brake smoothly. Mark where the front wheel lands.

Week 9A

Turning Sharp

New skill: lean into the turn. The bike follows the body. Don't yank the bars.

Week 10B

Cone Slalom Race

Weave through 5 cones, then back. Daddy times it. Beat the record.

Week 11A

Shoulder Check

New skill: look back over your shoulder without swerving. Build to looking both directions.

Week 12B

Look-and-Hold

Ride straight. Daddy holds up fingers behind you. Look back, call out the number, keep going straight.

Week 13A

Hand Signals

New skill: left turn (arm out), right turn (arm bent up), stop (arm bent down). Practice each.

Week 14B

Signal & Turn

Ride toward an intersection (or driveway). Look. Signal. Turn. In that order.

Week 15A

Shifting Gears

New skill: shift to a LOW gear (easier) before a hill goes up. HIGH gear for flat/downhill.

Week 16B

Gear Practice

Ride a flat section. Practice shifting up. Practice shifting down. Don't shift while standing still.

Week 17A

Standing Pedal

New skill: lift your butt off the seat and pedal STANDING for power on hills.

Week 18B

Stand Up Sprint

Stand up, pedal hard, accelerate. Sit back down to cruise.

Week 19A

Smooth Pedal Strokes

New skill: think of pedaling as drawing circles. Push DOWN at 2 o'clock. Pull THROUGH at the bottom. Up at 8 o'clock.

Week 20B

One-Foot Drill

Pedal with one foot only, 10 seconds. Switch. Builds the smooth circle.

Week 21A

Riding Slow

New skill: balance at a crawl. Lots of pedal pressure, eyes UP, light hands.

Week 22B

Slow Race

LAST one to finish wins. Stay on the bike. Don't put a foot down.

Week 23A

Hill Climbing

New skill: low gear, steady pedaling, eyes up at the top of the hill, not at the ground.

Week 24B

Pick a Hill

Find a small hill. Climb it. Coast back. Climb again. Build endurance.

Week 25A

Descending Control

New skill: feather the brakes (squeeze and release) going down hills. Don't lock them up.

Week 26B

Path Day

End-of-phase celebration. Ride the Mattapoisett Bike Path with Daddy. Full ride, water break, treat.

Phase 3 · Adding Match Play

Weeks 27–39 A, B & C rotating Real scoring arrives

Format C enters. Now three formats rotate (A → B → C → A → B → C…). Format C is where real small-sided games happen. Format A keeps introducing new skills (reading defenses, stiff-arms, play calling). Format B keeps the cooperative drill muscle. Format C is where Nicolas practices the regulation challenge of competitive games with built-in safety: champion moments, water breaks, predictable structure.

Week 27C

First Neighborhood Ride

Format C debuts. Ride to a near landmark with Daddy. Practice hand signals at every turn.

Week 28A

Reading the Road

New skill: scan ahead 20+ feet. Spot potholes, cars, walkers BEFORE you reach them.

Week 29B

Scan & Call

While riding, call out things you see: "pothole left!", "walker ahead!". Builds awareness.

Week 30C

Intersection Practice

Find a quiet intersection. Practice: slow down, look LEFT-RIGHT-LEFT, signal, cross.

Week 31A

Riding in Formation

New skill: single file behind Daddy. Stay 5 feet back. Match his speed.

Week 32B

Follow the Leader

Daddy leads. You follow. He calls signals; you echo them.

Week 33C

Bike to a Destination

Ride to an actual place — the library, the harbor, a friend's house. Real outing, not a loop.

Week 34A

Riding on Trails

New skill: dirt is different. Lighter grip. Stay relaxed. Eyes up the trail.

Week 35B

First Trail Ride

Find a flat dirt trail. Ride for 15 minutes. Stop and look around.

Week 36C

Longer Ride

Bigger route. 5–7 miles. Water break in the middle. Snack at the end.

Week 37A

Riding in Light Rain

New skill: brakes work LESS in the wet. Brake EARLIER. Slow down on turns. Bright clothes.

Week 38B

Drizzle Ride

Pick a light-drizzle day. Short ride. Practice the slower braking and turning.

Week 39C

Cape Canal Bikeway

End-of-phase celebration. Ride the Cape Cod Canal Bikeway with Daddy. 1–2 hours. Big-kid ride.

Phase 4 · Becoming a Rider

Weeks 40–52 A, B, C & D rotating Ownership emerges

Format D enters. All four rotate (A → B → C → D → A → B → C → D…). Format D is where Nicolas becomes the author. He picks his warm-up. He picks the skill to refine. He reflects at the end. The other formats keep doing what they do — but now there's a session every fourth week where he's running the show.

Week 40D

I Plan My Ride

Format D debuts. Nicolas picks the route. Looks at a map. Plans water stops.

Week 41A

Basic Maintenance

New skill: how to pump up a tire. How to read tire pressure (PSI on the side of the tire).

Week 42B

Pump & Pre-Ride

Pump both tires to the right PSI before every ride. Then go.

Week 43C

Multi-Stop Ride

Plan a ride with 2 stops (post office, ice cream, etc.). Lock the bike at each.

Week 44D

I Lead the Ride

Tactic: Nicolas leads, Daddy follows. Calls the signals.

Week 45A

Fixing a Flat

New skill: how to spot a flat. (For now, just identify it — actual fixing comes later.)

Week 46B

Pre-Ride Inspection

Look at the chain. Spin the wheels. Check tires for cuts. Brakes squeeze fully? Then go.

Week 47C

Longer Ride with Friends

Invite a cousin or neighbor. Group ride. Hand signals matter more with a group.

Week 48D

Riding Etiquette

Tactic: practice "On your left!" while passing walkers. Wave to other riders.

Week 49A

Bike Camping Concepts

New idea: how a bike could carry stuff. Panniers. Rear racks. The concept of a long ride with gear.

Week 50B

Loaded Bike Drill

Strap a small backpack of weight to the back rack. Ride with it. Notice how it feels different.

Week 51C

Big Ride

Best of the year — a longer ride to a destination. Bring snacks. Take pictures.

Week 52D

Biking Birthday

Year-end. Reflect: 3 favorite rides. Set a goal for Year 2. Celebrate.

Slow Race CLASSIC

The backyard bike classic. Two players line up. Both start at the same time. LAST one to cross the finish line wins. You have to stay ON the bike — feet off the ground. Builds balance, control, and patience.

Why it's perfect for the brothers: no speed pressure, built-in regulation breaks, the slower kid wins (great equalizer for different ages), and Alexander and Nicolas can both win on different days.

"Slower is harder."

Make any drill more fun

  • Add a story. "You're a Tour de France rider. The peloton is right behind you."
  • Add silly cues. "Pedal like there's lava on the ground."
  • Add a target. A cone, a chalk square. Aim is more fun than just riding around.
  • Add a count. "How many figure-8s in 60 seconds?" Counting builds rhythm.
  • Add a victory move. Hands up at the finish line. Yell "VICTORY!"
  • Add music. Bring a small speaker on a longer ride. Pedal to the beat.

Nicolas Goes for a Ride

A story about my lesson

🎾
PAGE 1My name is Nicolas. I am a biker.
📅
PAGE 2Today I am going for a bike ride. I will ride with Daddy.
🎒
PAGE 3I pack my bag. Racquet. Water bottle. Snack. I am ready.
🚗
PAGE 4I put on my helmet. Daddy checks the straps.
👋
PAGE 5I see Coach. He says "Hi Nicolas!" I can say hi back, or wave, or smile.
🤸
PAGE 6First we warm up. We jog. We stretch. My body wakes up.
🏃
PAGE 7Then we do drills. Coach feeds me balls. I hit them.
💪
PAGE 8Sometimes I hit it great. I feel proud.
🤔
PAGE 9Sometimes I miss. That is OK. Even pros miss balls. Missing means I am learning.
😮‍💨
PAGE 10If I feel frustrated, I can take a deep breath. I can ask for a water break. That is allowed.
🎯
PAGE 11Coach helps me. He gives me a tip. I try again.
🎮
PAGE 12At the end, we play a fun game. I love this part.
🤝
PAGE 13When the lesson is done, I shake Coach's hand. I say "Thank you Coach!"
⭐
PAGE 14I tell Mom or Dad one thing I liked. One thing I learned. One thing that was hard.
🌟
PAGE 15Biking makes me strong. Biking makes me brave. Biking makes me happy. I am a biker.
Falling is part of biking. You get back up. Brush off. Try again.. Learning is part of the game. Showing up is part of the game. You belong on the field.

How to Add a New Song

Tap the green + Add a Suno Song button at the top of this panel. The app will save your songs right on this iPad — they'll be there next time too.

  1. Make the song on Suno together with Nicolas/Alexander.
  2. Copy the share link from Suno (the URL of the song page).
  3. Tap + Add a Suno Song above.
  4. Enter the title, paste the URL, tap Save.

When to use the songs

🎒 On the way to practice

In the car. Builds anticipation. Lets the body get into "ride mode" before a ride.

🤸 Warm-up block

Play a song while doing menu moves. The song's length signals when warm-up is done.

💧 Between blocks

A 30-second song clip resets attention before moving to the next part of the lesson.

🏆 Champion moment

End every Format C lesson with a song. Pair the song with the win-feeling.

😮‍💨 Regulation

If a moment feels hard, pause and listen to a familiar song. Then come back to the field.

🚗 Drive home

Replay the song on the way home. Pairs the music with the memory of a good game.

A song is a feeling you can put in your pocket. Biking plus music plus rhythm plus joy — that is your ride.
